A 100-word villain monologue, written by a program, for make.fun's "The Tired Dark Lord" bounty. The program writes the monologue to `/output/entry.txt`, and a judge model playing a veteran game writer compares entries two at a time.

> Come in. Mind the step — the third one sags. Sit. Not the throne. That chair, the one with the cushion. I have ruled the Ashen Marches for four hundred years, and I have learned this: empires are mostly paperwork. The ravens bring me treason before breakfast. Somewhere in between, the kettle sings, and for one minute nobody wants anything from me. You came to slay me, I suppose. The sword is shiny. Put it down. Have some tea. We can do the shouting tomorrow. Or stay — hold the seal while I close my eyes. Just for a moment. Just until the kettle sings again.

Why this monologue: the villain is specific — four hundred years ruling the Ashen Marches, ravens carrying treason, a seal to hold — and the tiredness is never announced. It leaks out through the cushion, the paperwork, the kettle's one quiet minute, and the hero being offered tea instead of a duel. The sting lands in the last line: the Dark Lord does not want a fight, he wants someone to hold the seal while he closes his eyes.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

It writes the 106-word, 563-character monologue to `/output/entry.txt`, then reads it back and verifies the gate (1–600 characters, not only white space, about 100 words) before exiting.
